Level Up Program

I designed a web application that helps student employees track training progress and helps administrators manage learning objectives and advancement.

The existing process

The original process relied on an individual Leveling Document for each student employee. Supervisors and students used these documents to record training, review progress, and decide when a student was ready to advance.

The problem and the goal

More than 200 individual documents

  • Template changes required manual updates to each student’s document.
  • Administrators could not efficiently sort or edit profile information.
  • Hiring a group of students meant creating accounts one at a time.
  • The self-reporting process was difficult for student employees to use.

A shared dashboard connected to a database

The core scope covered student profiles and an administrator dashboard for viewing progress, department statistics, and training templates.

Additional priorities included advancement between levels, integrated quizzes, clearer progress charts, and visible training status.

Research

User and stakeholder interviews

I conducted more than five interviews with student employees, managers, and human resources staff. I focused on the tasks each person needed to complete and the limitations of the existing process.

Personas

I created three personas. Cyrus represented a human resources staff member responsible for onboarding new hires, approving training, and handling employee separation paperwork.

Design and iteration

Wireframes

I translated interview findings into wireframes that outlined the page structure. I then brought the wireframes back to participants for feedback in a second round of interviews.

Information architecture

After the second interview round, I mapped the website’s structure and navigation. This gave stakeholders and developers a shared view of the planned features and how they connected.

Prototype and handoff

I used the research and wireframe feedback to design the application prototype, then handed the design to developers.
