Understanding the shopping experience

Art Supply Warehouse is an art supplies retailer in Westminster, California. Our academic project examined its online shopping experience and proposed improvements to navigation, product discovery, and feedback after user actions.

Research

Competitive and comparative analysis

We compared the shopping experience with art retailers such as JOANN and Michaels, as well as broader retailers such as Amazon and Walmart. The comparison helped us understand how other stores presented products and supported online shopping.

Personas

We created personas to clarify users’ behaviors and needs. Nick, an art student at UC San Diego, represented shoppers balancing quality and affordability when buying supplies.

Ten interviews

We conducted ten 30-minute interviews about participants’ backgrounds, use of technology, and online shopping habits. The conversations helped us understand what shoppers look for and where they encounter friction.

Twenty-four survey responses

Our survey explored online shopping frequency, behaviors, and attitudes. We received 24 responses, including anonymous responses collected through Facebook.

Cognitive walkthrough

We evaluated the steps shoppers took to complete key tasks, looking for clear navigation, understandable layouts, and feedback after each action.

Usability testing

Five potential users completed six tasks while thinking aloud. We recorded their screens and comments to understand their decisions and identify points of confusion.

  • Create an account.
  • Search for a product and add it to the cart.
  • Purchase an item.
  • Create a shopping list.
  • Find an existing list and add one of its items to the cart.
  • Find the available shipping methods and fees.

What we learned

Shopping priorities

  • Clear product presentation helped participants evaluate items.
  • Difficulty finding products could lead shoppers to try another website.
  • Reviews influenced purchase decisions.
  • Customer service shaped perceptions of the shopping experience.
  • Relevant offers were welcome; pop-up ads were not.

Usability problems

  • Weak visual hierarchy made search and account links easy to overlook.
  • Broad search results did not match participants’ expectations.
  • Missing confirmations left participants unsure whether actions had succeeded.

Proposed feature: Pick-A-Pack

Pick-A-Pack was a concept for creating custom sets of reusable art supplies, such as pencils, pens, markers, and paints. We proposed it as a way to differentiate ASW from larger retailers; its effect on purchases would need further validation.

Evaluation and reflection

We performed a heuristic evaluation of the redesign to assess its clarity and usability against interface design principles. The evaluation indicated improvements over the original experience, while also identifying areas that still needed refinement.

This project connected research findings to concrete interface changes. Further usability testing would help evaluate the proposed shopping flows and Pick-A-Pack concept.
