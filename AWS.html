Two different dashboard needs

The project explored how the dashboard could better support both first-time setup and ongoing contact center management. The research questioned whether a shared page was meeting the needs of users with different roles and permissions.

Research and synthesis

I conducted semi-structured interviews with five solutions architects, mapped first-time and returning customer journeys, and compared onboarding and dashboard patterns across other products.

  • How did customers use the dashboard initially and over time?
  • Which setup steps were relevant to different roles?
  • What prevented customers from using the available metrics?

Key findings

Setup and daily work serve different users

Interviewees described separate responsibilities for deployment, administration, development, and monitoring. A static setup guide could be irrelevant to users without the role or permissions to complete its steps.

Metrics need visibility and flexibility

Interviewees described limited metric options, a lack of visualizations, and metrics placed below the fold. Some customers used other tools to build the views they needed.

The setup guide was most useful during initial onboarding. The research suggested a need to distinguish setup guidance from the information returning users needed for daily work.

Concept directions and next steps

The conceptual work explored separating setup guidance from metrics, adapting tutorials to completion and permissions, and making operational information easier to scan.

The proposed next steps were to review prototypes with solutions architects, refine tutorials and metric visualizations, and test the concepts with customers. The work culminated in research recommendations and conceptual prototypes.
